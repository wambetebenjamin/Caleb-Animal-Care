Kenya treats rabies as the public-health emergency it is: most cases a year end in human deaths, almost all from dog bites. The legal framework (including the Rabies Control Act and county bylaws such as Nairobi's dog-control rules) requires dogs to be vaccinated by a registered veterinarian and kept under control in public.

## What "compliant" looks like

1. **First rabies shot at 12–16 weeks** for puppies and kittens.
2. **Booster per certificate** — annually for most Kenyan-market vaccines; some 3-year formulations are available.
3. **Keep the yellow card.** Boarding kennels, transporters and county rangers ask for it. In a bite incident, that card is the difference between a quarantine observation and a destroyed dog.

## If someone is bitten

Wash the wound with soap under running water for 15 minutes and get to a hospital the same day for post-exposure prophylaxis. Then report the bite — us, and the county veterinary office. Rabies control is a public duty, not a private inconvenience.
