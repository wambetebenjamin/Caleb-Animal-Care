The question "is kibble better than home food?" has the wrong premise. The right question is whether the bowl is *balanced* — and most bowls in Nairobi are not, in one direction or the other.

## The balanced-kenyan-kitchen bowl

A solid home-cooked dog meal is roughly: **half protein** (boiled/ungspiced beef, omena, eggs, offal in rotation), **a quarter carbohydrate** (rice well cooked, sweet potato — skip salty githeri seasoned with onions, which are toxic), and **a quarter vegetables** (pumpkin, carrot, sukuma stems removed). Add a teaspoon of vegetable oil and a calcium source (crushed eggshell at a pinch) — that last step is the one everyone forgets.

Never feed: bones that splinter (cooked chicken bones are the classic foreign-body surgery), onions, garlic, grapes, chocolate, xylitol-sweetened peanut butter, or avocado.

## If you feed kibble

Budget kibbles bulk with maize; premium is not always better but *consistent* is. Check the bag for a named protein as the first ingredient, store it sealed in a jerrycan against our humidity, and weigh it — "the yellow cup" is how Labradors get to 45 kg.

## Body condition beats brand names

Feel your dog's ribs monthly: easy to feel under a thin fat layer, but not visible at rest. From above you should see a waist — not a barrel. Bring your dog to our free monthly weigh-ins and we'll log the trend in your portal.
