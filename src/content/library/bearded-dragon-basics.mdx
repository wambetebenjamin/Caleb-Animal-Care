Most "sick" bearded dragons we examine are not sick — they are cold, calcium-deficient, or both. Reptile medicine in Kenya is 80% husbandry, 20% prescription.

## The non-negotiable setup

- **Basking zone at 38–42°C** on one end of the vivarium, a cool end around 26°C. A thermometer at both ends — guessing doesn't count.
- **UVB tube (10.0 minimum)** spanning two-thirds of the enclosure, replaced every 6–9 months even while it still glows. Kenya's sunshine helps only if the dragon actually sits in it — glass blocks UVB.
- **Calcium dust** on feeder insects 4–5 times a week for juveniles, 3 times for adults, plus a multivitamin weekly.

## Feeding by age

1. **Juvens (under a year):** mostly protein — gut-loaded crickets, dubia roaches and the occasional waxworm treat. Fresh greens offered daily even if ignored.
2. **Adults (over a year):** flip the ratio — 70% dark leafy vegetables (sukuma, dandelion, butternut), 30% insects. Spinach binds calcium; skip it.

## Red flags that need an exotic vet

Jerky or wobbly walking and a soft, bendy jaw (metabolic bone disease), straining with no droppings (impaction from loose substrates like sand), or a gaping mouth away from the basking spot (respiratory infection). Bring photos of the enclosure to your appointment — the diagnosis is usually in the setup.
