Nairobi is a city that shares space with wildlife — raptors, monkeys, hyraxes, genets, and the larger neighbours from the Park and the conservancies. Well-meaning rescues often do more harm than the original injury. Two rules carry almost every situation.

## Rule one: contain, don't cuddle

1. Approach slowly; most injured wild animals will still try to bite, kick or claw.
2. Cover the animal with a large towel or blanket, then lift it into a ventilated cardboard box with air holes.
3. Keep the box **dark, quiet and warm** — darkness lowers wild-animal heart rates and stress hormones dramatically.
4. Do not feed or give water. Shock causes more deaths than missing a meal, and forced water can flood an injured animal's lungs.

## Rule two: call professionals, fast

- Call us on **+254 112 272 061** — our exotics vet treats urban wildlife through our conservancy partnerships and coordinates handovers.
- For big casualties (zebra, lion, giraffe), call the Kenya Wildlife Service line directly; moving large wildlife without a KWS permit is unlawful.
- Baby animals that seem "abandoned" often are not — fledgling birds on the ground usually have parents nearby. Contain only if there is obvious injury or immediate danger (cats, roads).

## And a note on photos

Everyone wants a selfie with the owl. Skip it. Every minute of human handling subtracts from the animal's recovery odds. The best story you'll tell is the one we release tomorrow.
