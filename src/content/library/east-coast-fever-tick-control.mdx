If you keep cattle near Nairobi, East Coast Fever is not an "if" — it is a "which season". The parasite arrives with the brown ear tick, and an untreated animal can die within three weeks of infection.

## Three lines of defence

1. **Tick control (the engine):** hand-spray or dip cattle every 5–7 days in wet seasons, every 10–14 days dry. Rotate acaricide active ingredients annually to slow resistance. Spray ears, dewlap, udder and tail switch — that's where ticks cluster.
2. **Infection-and-treatment immunity:** the ECF "live vaccine" (Muguga cocktail) given once by a registered vet confers strong long-term immunity. We administer it with oxytetracycline cover during herd visits.
3. **Grazing hygiene:** rest paddocks, cut grass longer than ankle height where possible, and don't mix newly bought animals with your herd for 3 weeks.

## Spotting ECF early

- Fever above 40°C and swollen lymph nodes in front of the shoulder
- Clear discharge from eyes and nose, then laboured breathing
- Loss of appetite and a sudden milk drop in dairy cows

> Suspected ECF? Treat with buparvaquone **at once** — waiting "to see" is the expensive option. Call us; we keep stock on the mobile unit.

## Calves first

Priority vaccination goes to calves between 4 weeks and 6 months. An adult cow that grew up immune rarely needs help — a naive bought-in animal always does.
