Emergencies never book appointments. A small, ready kit buys you the minutes that matter — stabilise, then call us on the 24-hour line.

## The 12-essentials checklist

1. Digital thermometer and water-based lubricant (dog fever is 39.2°C+; cat 39.4°C+)
2. Gauze squares and a self-adhesive bandage roll for bleeding
3. Saline solution for eyes and wound flushing
4. Styptic powder or cornflour for torn nails
5. Blunt tweezers and a tick hook — never squeeze a tick's body
6. Antiseptic: dilute povidone-iodine (tea colour) — **never** hydrogen peroxide in deep wounds
7. 3% hydrogen peroxide kept *only* for vet-instructed vomiting induction — call first
8. A clean muzzle or strip of cloth (a hurt dog bites the hand it loves)
9. Elizabethan collar or a folded towel to stop wound licking
10. Blanket for warmth and a sturdy carrier or flat board for transport
11. Oral syringe for flushing mouths after suspected poison contact
12. Our card with the emergency number: **+254 112 272 061**

## When first aid stops

Bloat (a hard, swollen abdomen with unproductive retching), toxin ingestion (rat poison, snail bait, human painkillers), seizures longer than 5 minutes, heatstroke, snakebite, and any breathing difficulty: these are call-now, drive-now cases. Don't treat these at home beyond keeping the animal cool, quiet and warm.
