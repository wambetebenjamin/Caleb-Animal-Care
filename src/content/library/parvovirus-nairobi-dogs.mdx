Parvovirus is the single most common killer of unvaccinated puppies we see at the clinic, and cases reliably spike after the long and short rains in Nairobi. The virus survives in soil and on surfaces for months, so a puppy can catch it from a pavement sniff in Kilimani as easily as from a sick dog.

## Early signs to act on today

- Sudden loss of appetite and low energy
- Vomiting, then diarrhoea — often bloody and foul-smelling
- Fever, then a dangerous drop in body temperature
- Dehydration: tacky gums, sunken eyes

> If your puppy has vomiting plus diarrhoea and is unvaccinated, treat it as an emergency. Every hour matters — call us before you drive in so we can prep isolation.

## Prevention beats treatment

1. Vaccinate at **6–8 weeks**, booster at 10–12 and 14–16 weeks.
2. Keep puppies off public ground until one week after their second shot.
3. Disinfect shoes at your gate if you have just handled a sick dog — the virus rides on soles.

## What treatment looks like

There is no drug that kills parvo; treatment is intensive support — IV fluids, anti-nausea medication, antibiotics against secondary infection and warmth. Survival rises sharply with early admission. Our isolation ward takes parvo cases 24/7.
