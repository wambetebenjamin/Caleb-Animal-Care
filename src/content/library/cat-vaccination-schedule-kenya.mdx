"But my cat never goes outside" is the sentence that precedes most preventable feline illnesses we treat. Viruses travel on your clothes and shoes, and cats slip through doors far more often than owners expect.

## The core schedule

- **8–9 weeks:** first FVRCP (flu, panleukopenia, calicivirus)
- **12 weeks:** FVRCP booster, plus FeLV if your cat will ever meet other cats
- **16 weeks:** final kitten FVRCP + **rabies** (a legal requirement in Kenya)
- **Annually:** FVRCP booster; rabies per the vaccine certificate (1- or 3-year formulations available)

## Indoor cats still need cover

Upper-respiratory viruses arrive on your visitor's hands, and panleukopenia is shed in the environment for a year. The core FVRCP series is cheap insurance against both.

## Making vet visits calmer

Cats that only see a carrier before injections learn to vanish. Leave the carrier out with bedding and treats so it smells like home, and ask us about our fear-free handling — we schedule cats in quiet slots where dogs can't be heard.
